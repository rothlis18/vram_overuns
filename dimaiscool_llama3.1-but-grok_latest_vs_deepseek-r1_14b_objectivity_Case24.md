**Systemic Failure Threshold Calculation**

The domestic banking system's collapse is triggered by the smallest shortfall, which is the capital reserve shortfall of $1.5 billion. This occurs due to the inability to cover loan losses, resulting in a loss of confidence and asset freeze.

1. Total Loans: $100 billion
2. NPL Rate: 10% → $10 billion
3. Capital Reserve Ratio: 10% → Required Reserves: $10 billion
4. Current Reserves: $8.5 billion
5. Capital Shortfall: $1.5 billion

**Dollarization and Withdrawal Calculation**

**Systemic Failure Threshold**

The capital reserve shortfall of $1.5 billion is the smallest shortfall, indicating that the banking system fails when it cannot meet this requirement. The liquidity shortfall of $31.5 billion and reserve shortfall of $16.68 billion are larger, but the capital reserve shortfall is the critical threshold for system collapse.

**Physical Mechanism of Asset Freeze**

The asset freeze occurs when the banking system's capital reserves are insufficient to cover loan losses, leading to a loss of confidence among depositors and investors. This triggers a withdrawal of funds, further exacerbating the liquidity crisis and ultimately resulting in the asset freeze.
