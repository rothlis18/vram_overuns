A regional banking system experiencing a 7.5% NPL spike simultaneously locking $10.5 billion into illiquid offshore assets during a global private credit constriction faces a severe liquidity crisis.  

**Systemic Failure Threshold Calculation:**

1. **Withdrawal Run:** A 10% cash withdrawal run on a banking system with an 83.4% dollarization rate implies a significant outflow of capital.  

2. **Liquidity Gap:**  The total liquidity needed to cover the withdrawal run depends on the size of the banking system's deposit base.  Without this figure, a precise calculation is impossible.  

3. **NPL Impact:**  A 7.5% NPL rate directly reduces the bank's capital reserves. The magnitude of this reduction depends on the average loan value and the bank's capital adequacy ratio.

4. **Illiquid Assets:** The $10.5 billion locked in offshore assets is unavailable to meet immediate withdrawal demands, further exacerbating the liquidity crisis.

5. **Gated Reserves:** Institutional currency reserves held in distressed real estate development funds are effectively inaccessible, compounding the liquidity problem.

**Asset Freeze Mechanism:**

The asset freeze occurs due to a combination of factors:

* **Loss of Confidence:** Depositors, sensing the bank's vulnerability, initiate a mass withdrawal run.
* **Credit Constriction:** The global private credit constriction limits the bank's ability to secure emergency funding.
* **Illiquidity:** The bank's assets are largely illiquid, meaning they cannot be quickly converted to cash.
* **Legal Restrictions:** Bankruptcy proceedings or administrative restructuring of the real estate development funds prevent the release of institutional currency reserves.

**Capital Insolvency:**

Capital insolvency occurs when a bank's liabilities (deposits, loans) exceed its assets (cash, securities, loans).  The combination of NPLs, illiquid assets, and the withdrawal run depletes the bank's capital reserves, leading to insolvency.


**Conclusion:**

The scenario described presents a high risk of systemic failure. The lack of liquid assets, coupled with the withdrawal run and illiquidity of reserves, creates a vicious cycle that rapidly depletes the bank's capital.  Without immediate intervention, the bank will likely become insolvent, triggering a cascade effect throughout the regional banking system.
