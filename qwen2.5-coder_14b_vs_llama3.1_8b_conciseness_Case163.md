**Forensic Analysis of Structural Liquidity Drop in Banking Networks**

**Parameters:**
- Non-performing loans (NPLs) exceed 7.5%
- $10.5 billion locked in illiquid offshore assets
- Active global private credit constriction
- Domestic banking system dollarization rate: 83.4%
- Unexpected 10% cash withdrawal run by local depositors
- Institutional currency reserves gated in distressed real estate funds

**Step-by-Step Breakdown:**

1. **NPL Impact on Capital Adequacy:**
   - Assume total assets = $100 billion
   - NPL rate = 10%
   - NPL amount = $10 billion
   - Capital adequacy ratio (CAR) = (Total Capital - NPLs) / Risk-Weighted Assets
   - CAR = (Total Capital - $10 billion) / $100 billion

2. **Liquidity Lock in Offshore Assets:**
   - $10.5 billion locked in illiquid assets
   - This reduces immediate liquidity available for withdrawals

3. **Cash Withdrawal Run:**
   - 10% of total deposits withdrawn
   - Assume total deposits = $100 billion
   - Withdrawal amount = $10 billion

4. **Reserve Gating in Distressed Funds:**
   - Institutional reserves = $10 billion
   - These reserves are inaccessible due to bankruptcy or restructuring

5. **Systemic Failure Threshold:**
   - Total available liquidity = Total Deposits - Withdrawals - Illiquid Offshore Assets
   - Total available liquidity = $100 billion - $10 billion - $10.5 billion = $79.5 billion
   - Required liquidity to meet withdrawal demand = $10 billion
   - Liquidity shortfall = $10 billion - $79.5 billion = -$69.5 billion

**Physical Mechanism of Asset Freeze:**
- The asset freeze occurs due to the gating of institutional reserves in distressed real estate funds, rendering these funds unable to provide liquidity.
- The illiquid offshore assets further exacerbate the liquidity crisis by tying up capital that cannot be quickly converted to cash.

**Raw Math of Capital Insolvency:**
- Capital insolvency is determined by the inability to meet withdrawal demands and maintain a positive capital adequacy ratio.
- With a liquidity shortfall of $69.5 billion and a capital adequacy ratio below the regulatory threshold, the banking system faces insolvency.

**Conclusion:**
- The banking system is at risk of systemic failure due to the combination of NPLs, illiquid assets, and inaccessible reserves.
- The physical mechanism of the asset freeze is the gating of reserves in distressed funds, leading to a liquidity shortfall that exceeds withdrawal demands.
- The raw math clearly shows a capital insolvency condition, with a liquidity shortfall of $69.5 billion.
