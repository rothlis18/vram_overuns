**Objective:** Quantitative systems-engineering breakdown of structural liquidity drop when regional banking networks experience non-performing loan (NPL) spikes exceeding 7.5%, while concurrently locking $10.5 billion into illiquid offshore asset structures during an active global private credit constriction.

**Initial Capital:** $100 billion in total assets.

**NPL Impact:** Exceeding 7.5% NPLs reduces capital by $7.5 billion, leaving $92.5 billion.

**Offshore Asset Lock:** $10.5 billion in illiquid assets reduces liquid assets to $82 billion.

**Cash Withdrawal Run:** A 10% run ($10 billion) cannot be met due to frozen reserves, leading to a $72 billion liquid asset base.

**Final Capital:** $72 billion, insufficient to cover liabilities and meet regulatory requirements.

**Systemic Failure Threshold:**

1. **NPL Spike:**

3. **Cash Withdrawal Run:**
   - Cash Run: $10 billion (10% of $100 billion)
   - Liquid Assets Post-Run: $72 billion ($82 billion - $10 billion)

**Conclusion:**

The systemic failure threshold is reached when the combination of NPL spikes, illiquid offshore assets, and a cash withdrawal run leads to capital insolvency. The final liquid asset base of $72 billion is insufficient to cover liabilities and meet regulatory requirements, resulting in systemic failure.

- **NPLs:** Non-performing loans reduce the bank's capital base, directly impacting liquidity.
- **Offshore Assets:** Illiquid offshore assets are frozen, reducing the pool of available funds.
