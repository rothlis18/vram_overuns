**Forensic Analysis:**

Given:
- Total deposits: $100 billion
- Dollarization rate: 83.4%
- NPL spike: 7.5% of total deposits
- Offshore assets locked: $10.5 billion
- Cash withdrawal run: 10% of total deposits
- Institutional currency reserves completely gated inside distressed real estate development funds

**Calculations:**

1. **Dollar-denominated reserves:** $100 billion * 83.4% = $83.4 billion
2. **Non-performing loans (NPL):** $100 billion * 7.5% = $7.5 billion
3. **Liquid assets (excluding offshore assets):** $83.4 billion - $7.5 billion = $75.9 billion
4. **Total liquid assets:** $75.9 billion - $10.5 billion (offshore assets) = $65.4 billion
5. **Cash withdrawal demand:** $100 billion * 10% = $10 billion

**Analysis:**

- With $65.4 billion in liquid assets, the banking system can meet the $10 billion cash withdrawal demand without insolvency.
- The systemic failure threshold is not reached, as liquid assets exceed withdrawal demands.
- The banking system's reliance on dollarization and offshore assets increases its vulnerability to cash withdrawal runs and global private credit constrictions.
- The complete gating of institutional currency reserves inside distressed real estate development funds exacerbates this vulnerability, as it limits the system's ability to access these reserves during a cash crisis.

**Hard Metrics:**

- Liquid assets: $65.4 billion
- Cash withdrawal demand: $10 billion
- Liquidity surplus: $55.4 billion ($65.4 billion - $10 billion)
- Systemic failure threshold not reached
