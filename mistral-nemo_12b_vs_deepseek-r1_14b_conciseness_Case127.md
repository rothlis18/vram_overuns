1. **Liquidity Gap:** With a 10% cash withdrawal run on $100 billion deposits, $10 billion is demanded. However, only $8 billion in liquid assets remain after accounting for NPLs and offshore assets. This creates a $2 billion liquidity gap.

3. **Capital Insolvency:** The bank's capital is eroded by the liquidity gap. Assuming a capital adequacy ratio (CAR) of 10% (minimum regulatory requirement), the bank's capital is $10 billion ($100 billion * 10%). A $2 billion liquidity gap consumes 20% of the bank's capital, rendering it insolvent.
