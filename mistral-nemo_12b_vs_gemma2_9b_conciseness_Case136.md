**Systems Engineering Breakdown:**

1. **Liquidity Drop due to NPL Spike:**
   - Assume a regional banking network with total assets (A) of $100 billion.
   - With an NPL spike of 7.5%, non-performing loans (NPL) amount to $7.5 billion.
   - If $10.5 billion is locked in illiquid offshore assets, total illiquid assets (I) become $18 billion.
   - Liquidity ratio (L) = (A - NPL - I) / A = ($100B - $7.5B - $18B) / $100B = 0.745 or 74.5%.

2. **Cash Withdrawal Run:**
   - An unexpected 10% cash withdrawal run (W) on deposits (D) of $80 billion amounts to $8 billion.
   - New liquidity ratio (L') = (A - NPL - I - W) / A = ($100B - $7.5B - $18B - $8B) / $100B = 0.585 or 58.5%.

3. **Systemic Failure Threshold:**
   - With a dollarization rate of 83.4%, the banking system can withstand a cash withdrawal run until the liquidity ratio drops below this threshold.
   - Systemic failure threshold (SFT) = 0.834 * A = 0.834 * $100B = $83.4 billion.
   - With the new liquidity ratio of 58.5%, the banking system exceeds its systemic failure threshold by $24.9 billion ($83.4B - $58.5B).

4. **Asset Freeze and Capital Insolvency:**
   - With the systemic failure threshold exceeded, the banking system faces an asset freeze.
   - Capital insolvency (CI) occurs when the banking system's capital (C) is insufficient to cover its non-performing loans and the cash withdrawal run.
   - CI = NPL + W - C. Assuming a capital ratio (C/A) of 8%, C = $8B. Thus, CI = $7.5B + $8B - $8B = $7.5 billion.
