A 7.5% NPL spike in regional banking networks, coupled with $10.5 billion locked in illiquid offshore assets during global private credit constriction, creates a liquidity crunch.  An 83.4% dollarization rate amplifies this vulnerability. A 10% cash withdrawal run against a banking system with institutional currency reserves gated in distressed real estate funds triggers a complete freeze.  

The systemic failure threshold is reached when the 10% withdrawal demand exceeds the remaining liquid assets.  This results in a capital insolvency event.  The physical mechanism of the asset freeze is the inability of banks to meet withdrawal demands due to illiquidity.  The resulting capital insolvency is a direct consequence of the mismatch between deposit liabilities and available liquid assets.
