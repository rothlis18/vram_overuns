**SYSTEMIC LIQUIDITY DROPS AND CAPITAL INSOLVENCY**

Given the scenario of regional banking networks experiencing non-performing loan (NPL) spikes exceeding 7.5%, concurrent locking of $10.5 billion into illiquid offshore asset structures, and an active global private credit constriction, we will calculate the systemic failure threshold for a domestic banking system with an 83.4% dollarization rate.

**PHYSICAL MECHANISM OF ASSET FREEZE**

When NPLs exceed 7.5%, banks experience a significant increase in loan defaults, leading to a decrease in available liquidity. Concurrently, locking $10.5 billion into illiquid offshore asset structures further reduces the bank's ability to meet its short-term obligations. This creates a liquidity crisis, where the bank's assets become illiquid due to the lack of marketability and the inability to quickly convert them into cash.

**RAW MATH OF CAPITAL INSOLVENCY**

Assuming a domestic banking system with an 83.4% dollarization rate, where 83.4% of deposits are denominated in foreign currency, and the remaining 16.6% are in local currency. If an unexpected 10% cash withdrawal run is executed by local depositors, but institutional currency reserves are completely gated inside distressed real estate development funds undergoing bankruptcy or administrative restructuring, the bank's liquidity crisis worsens.

**CALCULATIONS**

1. **Available Liquidity**: Assuming a bank with $100 billion in deposits, and 83.4% dollarization rate, the bank has $83.4 billion in foreign currency deposits. With an NPL spike exceeding 7.5%, the bank's available liquidity decreases by 10% (7.5% NPL + 2.5% buffer), resulting in $74.97 billion in available liquidity.
2. **Illiquid Assets**: With $10.5 billion locked into illiquid offshore asset structures, the bank's available liquidity decreases by an additional 10.5% ($10.5 billion / $100 billion), resulting in $66.87 billion in available liquidity.
3. **Currency Reserve Gating**: Assuming the bank has $5 billion in institutional currency reserves, but these reserves are completely gated inside distressed real estate development funds, the bank's available liquidity decreases by an additional 5% ($5 billion / $100 billion), resulting in $63.42 billion in available liquidity.
4. **10% Cash Withdrawal Run**: If local depositors execute a 10% cash withdrawal run, the bank's available liquidity decreases by an additional 10% ($6.34 billion / $63.42 billion), resulting in $56.71 billion in available liquidity.

**SYSTEMIC FAILURE THRESHOLD**

Based on the calculations above, the systemic failure threshold for the domestic banking system is reached when available liquidity falls below 10% of total deposits. In this scenario, the bank's available liquidity falls to $56.71 billion, which is 56.71% of total deposits. This exceeds the 10% threshold, indicating a high likelihood of systemic failure.

The physical mechanism of the asset freeze is the combination of NPL spikes, illiquid offshore asset structures, and currency reserve gating, which reduces the bank's available liquidity and increases the risk of capital insolvency.
