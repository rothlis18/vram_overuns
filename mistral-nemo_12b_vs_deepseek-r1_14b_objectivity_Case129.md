**Systems Engineering Breakdown:**

1. **Initial Conditions:**
   - Regional banking networks have a 7.5% NPL spike.
   - $10.5 billion is locked in illiquid offshore assets.
   - Dollarization rate is 83.4%.
   - Institutional currency reserves are trapped in distressed real estate development funds.

2. **Cash Withdrawal Run:**
   - An unexpected 10% cash withdrawal run is executed by local depositors.
   - This means that depositors attempt to withdraw 10% of their total deposits.

3. **Asset Freeze Mechanism:**
   - With the currency reserves gated inside distressed real estate development funds, banks cannot liquidate these assets to meet the cash withdrawal demand.
   - This leads to an immediate asset freeze, where depositors cannot access their funds.

4. **Capital Insolvency Calculation:**
   - Let's assume the total deposits (D) in the banking system are $100 billion (for ease of calculation).
   - A 10% cash withdrawal run means depositors attempt to withdraw $10 billion (0.10 * $100 billion).
   - However, the banks only have $8 billion in liquid assets (100% - 7.5% NPL - $10.5 billion offshore assets = $8 billion).
   - This leaves a shortfall of $2 billion ($10 billion withdrawal demand - $8 billion liquid assets).
   - Therefore, the banks are insolvent by $2 billion, and they cannot meet the cash withdrawal demand.

5. **Systemic Failure Threshold:**
   - The systemic failure threshold is reached when the cash withdrawal demand exceeds the liquid assets by more than the banks' capital buffer.
   - In this case, the banks have no capital buffer left after the 7.5% NPL spike and the $10.5 billion locked in offshore assets.
   - Therefore, the systemic failure threshold is reached when the cash withdrawal demand exceeds $8 billion.

6. **Macroeconomic Impact:**
   - A systemic failure in the banking system would lead to a loss of confidence in the banking sector, potentially causing a bank run.
   - This could lead to a freeze in credit markets, slowing down economic activity and potentially pushing the region into a recession.
   - The dollarization rate of 83.4% exacerbates this issue, as it limits the central bank's ability to provide liquidity in local currency.
