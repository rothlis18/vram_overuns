**Forensic Analysis of Structural Liquidity Drop in Regional Banking Networks**

**1. Non-Performing Loan (NPL) Spikes and Illiquid Offshore Assets**

- **NPL Spike Impact**: When NPLs exceed 7.5%, regional banking networks experience a significant reduction in capital adequacy ratios. This is because NPLs are considered a form of impaired assets, which reduces the bank's equity and, consequently, its ability to lend or absorb further losses.
  
- **Illiquid Offshore Assets**: Locking $10.5 billion into illiquid offshore asset structures during a global private credit constriction exacerbates the liquidity crisis. These assets are difficult to sell quickly, and their value may decline due to market uncertainty, further straining the bank's liquidity.

**2. Systemic Failure Threshold**

- **Dollarization Rate**: The banking system relies on an 83.4% dollarization rate, meaning that 83.4% of the currency in circulation is in US dollars. This high dollarization rate can lead to a severe liquidity crunch if the US dollar becomes scarce or if there is a sudden demand for dollars.

- **Cash Withdrawal Run**: An unexpected 10% cash withdrawal run by local depositors can significantly deplete the bank's physical cash reserves. If the reserves are completely gated inside distressed real estate development funds undergoing bankruptcy or administrative restructuring, the bank will be unable to meet this demand.

**3. Physical Mechanism of Asset Freeze**

- **Distressed Real Estate Funds**: These funds are likely heavily leveraged and have significant exposure to real estate assets. During a bankruptcy or restructuring, the funds may be unable to liquidate their assets quickly, leading to a freeze on capital. This freeze can prevent the bank from accessing its reserves, further exacerbating the liquidity crisis.

**4. Raw Math of Capital Insolvency**

- **Initial Capital**: Assume the banking system has $100 billion in total assets. With an 83.4% dollarization rate, $83.4 billion of these assets are in US dollars.

- **NPL Impact**: If NPLs exceed 7.5%, the bank's capital adequacy ratio is reduced. Assuming a 10% NPL rate, the bank's capital is reduced by $7.5 billion, leaving $92.5 billion in assets.

- **Offshore Asset Lock**: The $10.5 billion in illiquid offshore assets are frozen, reducing the bank's liquid assets to $82 billion.

- **Cash Withdrawal Run**: A 10% cash withdrawal run by local depositors requires $10 billion in physical cash. If the reserves are frozen in distressed real estate funds, the bank cannot meet this demand, leading to a capital insolvency.

- **Final Capital**: After the cash withdrawal run, the bank has $72 billion in liquid assets, which is insufficient to cover its liabilities and meet regulatory capital requirements.

**Conclusion**: The combination of NPL spikes, illiquid offshore assets, and a cash withdrawal run, compounded by the gating of reserves in distressed real estate funds, creates a systemic failure threshold that leads to capital insolvency. The physical mechanism of the asset freeze is the inability to liquidate distressed real estate assets, and the raw math demonstrates that the bank's liquid assets are insufficient to meet its obligations.
